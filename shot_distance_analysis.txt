OPTIMAL RANGE ANALYSIS
----------------------

  Recommended range    : 6 – 10 ft from hub center
  Commanded distance   : 8 ft (midpoint of sweet spot)
  Tolerance            : ±6 in (±0.5 ft)

WHY 6–10 FT?

  1. FLYWHEEL HEADROOM
     At 6 ft: 39.4 fps (39% of max). At 10 ft: 49.0 fps (49% of max).
     The flywheel is nowhere near its limit across the entire 2–30 ft range
     (89.2 fps at 30 ft). Distance is not what constrains the shooter —
     the limiting factors are entry angle, trajectory height, and cycle time.

  2. ENTRY ANGLE — 70–73° (nearly vertical)
     Above 6 ft the ball enters the hub at >70° from horizontal (steep, clean drop).
     At 2 ft the entry is only 48.6° — shallow, more likely to rattle out.
     The 70–73° window maximizes scoring consistency.

  3. TIME OF FLIGHT — 1.18–1.54 s
     Short enough that the velocity-compensation code in UltraShooter can
     meaningfully correct for robot motion. Above 20 ft, ToF exceeds 2.2 s
     and aim latency becomes a real accuracy concern.

  4. PEAK HEIGHT — 7.7–11.6 ft above hood (10–14 ft above floor)
     Well within venue ceiling clearance everywhere. At 20 ft the ball peaks
     22 ft above the hood (~24 ft off the floor); at 25 ft it peaks ~30 ft
     off the floor — marginal in low-ceiling venues.

  5. CYCLE TIME
     Closer position = less drive distance per cycle = more balls per match.
     6–10 ft is close enough to reload quickly while staying out of hub
     collision range.
